Currently, partner notification and other activities that could be interpreted as warnings are limited to health departments, while healthcare providers' duties focus on their patients. However, three states do impose legal duties on the healthcare provider/patient relationship for third parties, even if these states do not require a warning. These legal duties vary in each of the three states:

 California

"An effort, through the cooperation of the patient," should be made to bring the third party in for examination and treatment.
If the physician does not have evidence that the third party received treatment within 10 days, the physician must report the suspected case to the health department (17 CCR § 2636).

 Nebraska

If a patient identifies a third party as someone potentially exposed, this suspected case should be reported to the local or state health department.
Physicians should “make reasonable efforts to prevent the spread of disease to others,” which could include third party notification depending on the circumstances. However, 1-007.02B1 makes clear that the state or local health department perform partner notification and referrals, which means the provider’s duty is likely limited to health department reporting and actions directed to their patient (i.e., counseling and linkage to care and treatment) (Neb. Admin. R. & Regs. Tit. 173, Ch. 1, § 1-007).

 Indiana

 If a patient identifies a third party as someone potentially exposed, this “suspected case” should be reported to the local health officer (410 IAC 1-2.5-75).
