Cardiovascular disease (CVD) is the leading cause of morbidity and death in the US, resulting in more than 1 of every 4 deaths.1 Coronary heart disease is the single leading cause of death and accounts for 43% of deaths attributable to CVD in the US.2,3 In 2019, an estimated 558,000 deaths were caused by coronary heart disease and 109,000 deaths were caused by ischemic stroke. Men have a higher overall prevalence of and mortality from CVD, although women experience higher mortality from certain cardiovascular events, such as stroke. On average, men experience CVD events earlier in life compared with women. The prevalence of CVD also differs by race and ethnicity. Among both sexes, Black adults have the highest prevalence of CVD.4
