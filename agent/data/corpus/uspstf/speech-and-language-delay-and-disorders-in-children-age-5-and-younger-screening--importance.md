Speech and language delays and disorders can pose significant problems for children and their families. Evidence suggests that school-aged children with speech or language delays may be at increased risk of learning and literacy disabilities, including difficulties with reading and writing.1-3 Observational cohort studies suggest that children with these conditions may also be at higher risk for social and behavioral problems in addition to learning problems, some of which may persist through adulthood.4 Research is needed to determine whether identifying speech and language delays early (ie, in children 5 years or younger), and providing interventions helps prevent these issues before they interfere with school learning or psychosocial adjustment.
