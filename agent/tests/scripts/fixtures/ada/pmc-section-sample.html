11. Chronic Kidney Disease and Risk Management: Standards of Care in Diabetes—2026

Diabetes Care. 2025 Dec 8;49(Suppl 1):S239–S259. doi: 10.2337/dc26-S011

Abstract

The American Diabetes Association's Standards of Care in Diabetes includes ADA's current clinical practice recommendations and is intended to provide the components of diabetes care.

Epidemiology of Diabetes and Chronic Kidney Disease

Chronic kidney disease (CKD) occurs in 20–40% of people with diabetes and is the leading cause of end-stage kidney disease. Early detection and intervention reduce both kidney and cardiovascular risk.

Diabetes accounts for approximately half of all cases of kidney failure requiring kidney replacement therapy in the U.S.

Assessment of Albuminuria and Estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate

Recommendations

11.1a At least once a year, assess urinary albumin (e.g., spot urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio [UACR]) and estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) in people with type 1 diabetes with duration of ≥5 years and in all people with type 2 diabetes regardless of treatment. B

11.1b In people with established diabetes-related kidney disease, urinary albumin and eGFR should be monitored 1–4 times per year depending on stage of disease. B

Treatment for Severe Chronic Kidney Disease and Kidney Failure

For people with diabetes and stage 5 CKD or kidney failure, kidney replacement therapy (dialysis or transplantation) is indicated. Pancreas or pancreas-after-kidney transplant may be considered in selected individuals with type 1 diabetes.
