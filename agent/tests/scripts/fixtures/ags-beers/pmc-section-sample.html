American Geriatrics Society 2023 updated AGS Beers Criteria for potentially inappropriate medication use in older adults

J Am Geriatr Soc. 2023 Jul;71(7):2052-2081. doi: 10.1111/jgs.18372

Abstract

The American Geriatrics Society Beers Criteria has been updated for 2023. The Criteria are intended to serve as a clinical tool to improve medication safety in older adults.

INTRODUCTION

The Beers Criteria was developed by the late Mark Beers, MD, in 1991 with the purpose of identifying medications for which potential harm outweighed the expected benefit and that should be avoided in nursing home residents. The 2023 American Geriatrics Society (AGS) Beers Criteria® for Potentially Inappropriate Medication (PIM) Use in Older Adults is the seventh overall update.

The 2023 Criteria comprise five tables: a list of medications considered potentially inappropriate (Table 2), drug-disease and drug-syndrome interactions (Table 3), medications to be used with caution (Table 4), potentially inappropriate drug-drug interactions (Table 5), and medications whose dosages should be adjusted based on renal function (Table 6).

TABLE 2.

2023 American Geriatrics Society Beers Criteria for potentially inappropriate medication use in older adults.

Anticholinergics: First-generation antihistamines (diphenhydramine, hydroxyzine) — avoid; highly anticholinergic, clearance reduced with advanced age, risk of confusion, dry mouth, and other anticholinergic effects. Strength of recommendation: Strong.

Benzodiazepines (lorazepam, diazepam) — avoid; older adults have increased sensitivity and slower metabolism. May cause cognitive impairment, delirium, falls, fractures, motor vehicle crashes. Strength of recommendation: Strong.

TABLE 3.

Drug-disease or drug-syndrome interactions that may exacerbate the disease or syndrome.

Heart failure: Avoid NSAIDs and COX-2 inhibitors — promote fluid retention and exacerbate heart failure. Strength of recommendation: Strong.

METHODS

Panel composition

An interprofessional panel of 13 experts was convened by the American Geriatrics Society.

Literature review

The panel reviewed evidence published since the 2019 update through a systematic literature search of MEDLINE and EMBASE.

RESULTS

Noteworthy changes from the 2019 update include the deletion of short-acting nifedipine, addition of warfarin to drugs to avoid for atrial fibrillation in favor of direct oral anticoagulants, and updated guidance on the use of sulfonylureas.

DISCUSSION

The Beers Criteria are intended as a starting point for medication review, not a substitute for clinical judgment. Use the criteria as one input alongside the patient's preferences, goals of care, and clinical context.
